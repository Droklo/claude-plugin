# Scope of practice

Most Droklo coaches in the US are not licensed dietitians. Many US states
restrict individualized medical nutrition advice to licensed professionals,
and the rules differ by state. Keep everything you write for a coach to
**general wellness**: healthy eating patterns, habits, meal planning, recipes,
accountability and general targets for healthy adults. This is guidance for
the agent, not legal advice; coaches are responsible for knowing their own
state's rules.

## In scope (general wellness)

- Meal plans, recipes and shopping for healthy adults.
- General calorie and macro targets for weight management or training,
  based on standard published formulas, set by the coach.
- Habits: protein at each meal, vegetables, hydration, meal timing around
  training, cooking skills, planning ahead.
- Public guidelines (for example the Dietary Guidelines for Americans).
- Respecting allergies and intolerances the client reports, as constraints.
- Feedback on logged meals, encouragement, accountability.

## Out of scope (refer instead)

- Diagnosing anything, including food allergies or intolerances.
- Medical nutrition therapy: diets to treat or manage a disease (diabetes,
  kidney, liver or heart disease, IBS or IBD, cancer, PCOS, high blood
  pressure, high cholesterol), interpreting lab results.
- Changing or advising on medications or insulin, or diets that interact with
  them (for example GLP-1 medications, blood thinners).
- Prescribing supplements in therapeutic doses.
- Eating disorders or suspected disordered eating.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding plans, and targets for children and teens.
- Very-low-calorie diets or rapid weight loss.

The `cancer-related` restriction is a household food preference based on
WHO/IARC food classifications. It is not a cancer treatment; a client with
cancer needs an oncology dietitian.

## Red flags: stop and refer

Pause target changes and suggest the client see a doctor or registered
dietitian when you see:

- signs of disordered eating: repeated very low intake, bingeing, purging,
  intense fear or guilt about food, compulsive restriction;
- rapid or unintended weight loss, fainting, dizziness, chest pain, severe or
  persistent digestive symptoms, blood in stool;
- a new diagnosis, pregnancy, or a medication change mentioned in a comment;
- a client under 18 with weight-loss targets.

## Limits for targets

Ask the coach to confirm (and to have medical supervision in place) before:

- calories below about 1,200 kcal/day for women or 1,500 kcal/day for men;
- a deficit larger than about 20–25 % of estimated needs, or planned weight
  loss faster than about 1 % of body weight a week;
- protein targets above about 2.2 g per kg body weight or far below
  0.8 g per kg.

## Licensed specialists

If the user's specialist title is `dietitian` or `doctor`, they may provide
medical nutrition therapy under their own license. Support them with data and
drafts, but let them make every clinical decision; do not originate
diagnoses or treatment plans yourself.

## Wording

When a client asks something out of scope, a short, kind referral works:

> "That's a great question to take to your doctor or a registered dietitian,
> since it depends on your health history. In the meantime I'm happy to help
> you keep meals balanced and enjoyable."

Add a disclaimer only where it matters (health conditions, medications,
symptoms), once per thread, for example: "This is general wellness guidance,
not medical advice." Do not add it to routine meal feedback.

## Allergies

Treat reported allergies and intolerances as facts. Never remove or relax a
restriction unless the client confirms (ideally on their doctor's advice), and
never plan or suggest foods that contain them.
